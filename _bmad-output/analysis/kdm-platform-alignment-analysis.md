## Executive Summary

**Critical Finding:** There is a significant misalignment between the current platform implementation and KDM's actual business model. The platform was built assuming a scalable SaaS model with hundreds of users, but KDM operates a **boutique consortium model** with 12-50 highly curated members focused on delivering specific government contracts.

**Key Disconnect:** The developer (Brian) built for scale and automation, while the business (Keith) needs a **high-touch, relationship-driven platform** that supports manual curation and contract delivery.

## 4. HOW THE CONSORTIUM ACTUALLY WORKS

### Consortium Definition (Clarified)

**NOT:** A network marketing group or pyramid scheme  
**NOT:** A community of 300+ businesses  
**IS:** A finite group (12-50) of expert companies that:

1. **Meet weekly** (Fridays at 3pm) to track progress
2. **Bring opportunities** that fit consortium capabilities
3. **Collaborate on contracts** where KDM acts as prime or project manager
4. **Share revenue/equity** based on contract roles
5. **Contribute expertise** to win and deliver contracts

### Revenue Model Per Member Type

**Type 1: Consortium Members ($1,250/month)**
- Pay monthly membership
- Get curated opportunity access
- Participate in team assembly
- Access to buyer briefings
- 2 hours concierge support/month

**Type 2: Contract Partners (Revenue Share)**
- Companies like Lonnie Johnson who need public sector division
- KDM acts as project manager on their contracts
- Revenue share based on contract scope delivery
- Example: NASA STEM solicitation with Lonnie Johnson

**Type 3: Prime Contractor Partnerships (Fee-based)**
- Lockheed Martin CMMC partnership
- KDM delivers specific scope (CMMC certification support)
- Fixed fee or percentage of prime contract
- Example: $1M+ to help small businesses get CMMC certified

**Type 4: Sponsorships**
- Open Door Power Room with 12 largest CEOs
- Sponsorship fees for visibility and access
- Focus on US manufacturing + critical minerals

### Contract Delivery Model

**Example: Lockheed Martin CMMC Contract**
- **Prime:** Lockheed Martin
- **Face/Voice:** KDM (CMMC expertise)
- **Delivery Team:** E3S + expanded CMMC experts
- **Verticals:** CMMC in manufacturing vs. other sectors

**Example: Lonnie Johnson NASA Contract**
- **Technical Lead:** Lonnie Johnson (aerospace engineering)
- **Project Manager:** KDM (public sector division)
- **Reason:** Lonnie lacks revenue for JP Morgan requirements
- **Reason:** Lonnie lacks time/expertise for defense contracting process

## CONCLUSION

The KDM platform is technically sound but strategically misaligned. The core issue is a **business model mismatch**: the platform was built for a scalable SaaS model with hundreds of users, but KDM operates a boutique consortium model with 12-50 curated members focused on winning and delivering government contracts.

**The fix is not technical - it's strategic:**

1. **Reframe the platform** as a consortium enablement tool, not a mass-market SaaS product
2. **Simplify the messaging** to clearly explain the consortium model in 30 seconds
3. **Deploy the content** that already exists (Five Pillars videos, team bios, testimonials)
4. **Document the structure** through a formal alignment session
5. **Focus on contracts** as the primary revenue driver, not subscriptions
